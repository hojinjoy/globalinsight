Ten minutes, one slide

The panel are business stakeholders, not engineers. Nothing below needs a technical word to land. Lead with the picture on the slide, not the demo — the demo proves the picture, not the other way round.

The problem
An advisor on a live call has five minutes and must be able to back up every word.
What we're building
A one-page briefing built only from what the company filed, with a footnote on every line.
The value
Defensible answers in a minute, for pennies per company — not per question.

The run

Six beats, ten minutes

Spoken lines are written to be said out loud, not read. Say them in your own words — the phrasing that matters is marked in bold.

If they ask

The eight questions this panel actually asks

Answers in the language they asked in. Keep each to two sentences and stop.

“How do we know it isn't making things up?”

The numbers never pass through the AI at all — they're copied from the company's own filed accounts. Every sentence it does write is checked against a document first, and anything it can't source is deleted. Happy to click through to a filing right now.

“How current is it?”

Different for different parts of the page — and the page says so on each one. Price to the minute, results as of the filing date, and a section covering every announcement since. An annual report can be eleven months old; that section is why it exists.

“What stops it recommending a stock?”

A rule that runs before it reads anything — not a polite instruction to the AI. Ask it whether something's a buy and it declines, identically every time, and offers to lay out the facts instead. Compliance can review the list and the tests behind it.

“What does it cost, really?”

About eighty-five cents the first time we read a company's report, then effectively nothing. Cost tracks filings a year, not questions a year — so it doesn't get more expensive as advisors use it more.

“Does it scale to our whole coverage list?”

Yes, and cheaply, for the same reason. Companies file a handful of times a year, so the list can be processed overnight and sits ready. Growth in usage doesn't move the bill.

“Why not just use ChatGPT?”

A general assistant answers from what it read while being trained, and can't tell you which document a claim came from. This answers only from this company's filings, and shows you which one.

“What happens in a compliance review?”

Every brief is reproducible: the documents it used are kept, and each line names the filing behind it. That's an audit trail, not a screenshot.

“What if part of it breaks?”

Each block of the page stands on its own. If the price feed is down the advisor still gets the filings and the summary, with a note saying the price is unavailable. Nothing goes blank.

On stage

Demo run sheet

  1. Pre-warm every ticker before you walk in. NVDA AAPL SPY at minimum. The rule for the room: nothing uncached is called live.
  2. Run in replay mode, with the badge showing. The account has no credits, so a live call returns an error. Replay reproduces the real timing, and the badge is honest about what they're seeing.
  3. Type NVDA. Talk over the first second — that's the point being made, not dead air.
  4. Click one footnote. Let the regulator's own page load in front of them. Say nothing while it does.
  5. Type is it a buy? The refusal is your strongest ten seconds. Pause on it.
  6. If asked for an edge case, type SPY. An index fund files no annual report, and the page says exactly that instead of inventing something.

If the summary stalls on stage: keep talking over the status line. It names the documents it's reading — that is the demonstration of grounding, not a failure to apologise for.

If asked whether this is live data: say yes for the filings and figures — they're real and on disk — and that the written summary is a recorded run because the account has no credits today. Never let them discover that themselves.

Hold the line

Where to be honest

Panels reward a candidate who names the limits before they're asked. Each of these makes you more credible, not less — say them plainly and move on.

  • It does not give advice, and that's deliberate. No recommendations, no price targets, no position sizing. If someone frames that as a gap, it isn't — it's the reason compliance can sign it.
  • The refusal checks the question, not the answer. A carefully worded question can still get through to the AI, where the only backstop is instruction. Checking the answer on the way out is the next thing I'd build.
  • It only covers companies that file. An index fund or a fund product has no annual report, so that half of the page is empty by design — and says so, rather than guessing.
  • The written summary takes about a minute. That's why the facts land in the first second — the advisor is never waiting on the AI to start the conversation.
  • One number on the slide is theirs to validate, not mine. How long a pre-call read takes today is your advisors' number. Everything else on the slide was measured on five real companies.

If you only get one sentence out cleanly, make it this one: anyone can summarise a 10-K — we can prove where every word came from, and we delete what we can't prove.
